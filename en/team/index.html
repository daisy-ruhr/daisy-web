Project team¶

DAISY connects computer science, psychology and practice. The consortium comprises three scientific partners and one practice partner; four associated partners contribute as multipliers and dialogue partners without funding of their own.

Project partners¶

RUB – Ruhr University Bochum¶

Focus on the technical subsystem: security and data protection architectures of AIaaS systems, empirical system analysis, demonstrators.

Dr.-Ing. Steffen Becker¶

Technology

I lead DAISY’s technical subproject at Ruhr University Bochum. My research combines IT security and human-computer interaction. In DAISY, we examine the actual privacy risks of AI-as-a-Service systems and develop safeguards that are technically robust and practical in everyday work.

UW/H – Witten/Herdecke University¶

Organizational link between the social and the technical subsystem: decision-making and negotiation processes, process models for calibrated AIaaS introductions.

Dr. Alina Tausch¶

Organizational psychology

I am leading the organizational psychology component of the project, currently from the University of Witten/Herdecke. Drawing on my research at the intersection of work design and organizational psychology, I examine - as a business psychologist - how decision-makers in organizations decide about AI and what role organizational factors, such as organizational culture, play.

Lukas Bring¶

PhD - Organizational psychology

As a psychologist and research associate at Witten/Herdecke University within the DAISY project, I focus on the organizational psychology of decision-making when companies adopt AI-as-a-Service systems. I investigate the criteria decision-makers use when they select and introduce these systems, how they weigh opportunities against risks, and what role data protection and employees’ interests play in those decisions. The goal is to better understand how organizations can introduce AI systems responsibly, with care for data privacy and for the people who work with them.

UDE – University of Duisburg-Essen¶

Social subsystem and individual level: mental models of AIaaS users, intervention formats for competence development.

Dr. Greta Ontrup¶

Psychology

Together with Dr. Magdalena Wischnewski, I lead the project’s work and organizational psychology part. I bring expertise in human-AI teaming and in how data-driven business processes affect employees. In DAISY, I study how employees perceive systems delivered as a service. I focus especially on the risks that come from employees’ own beliefs and interpretations about data protection and privacy.

Dr. Magdalena Wischnewski¶

Psychology

I lead the junior research group on human factors in AI systems. In my research, I examine what it means to "trust AI". In the DAISY project, I contribute to the individual psychology perspective by investigating how employees make sense of intelligent systems and how this is reflected in their interaction with them.

Muazzez Deniz Barut¶

PhD - Psychology

I am a PhD researcher at the University of Duisburg-Essen, focusing on the psychological aspects of human-AI interaction. In DAISY, I investigate how employees understand AI-as-a-Service systems and the mental models they develop about how these systems work, as well as the associated privacy and security risks. The goal is to identify potential misconceptions and promote informed and privacy-conscious AI use in the workplace.

BoWE – WirtschaftsEntwicklungsGesellschaft Bochum mbH¶

Practice partner in a dual role: implements solutions operationally as pilot user and acts as a regional multiplier in Bochum's business community and city administration.

Gregor Mähler¶

Practice

I lead the DAISY project at Bochum Economic Development, focusing on secure, privacy-compliant AI-as-a-Service and its practical applications. Together with our university partners, I assess technical and organisational requirements and share the findings with other businesses.
